Site Rejection: Why Sites Get Rejected and How to Get Approved

Published on WorkAtAp | A complete guide to understanding and overcoming AdSense rejection

Getting rejected by Google AdSense is a frustrating experience, especially when you have worked hard on your website. However, rejection is not the end of the road. Every rejected site can be fixed and resubmitted for review. In this final episode, we cover the most common reasons for AdSense site rejection, how to read and understand Google's feedback, and the exact steps you need to take to get your site approved.

AdSense Rejection Is Common and Fixable

It is important to understand that AdSense rejection is extremely common, particularly for first-time applicants. Many successful publishers were rejected multiple times before getting approved. Rejection does not mean your site is bad — it means there are specific issues that need to be addressed before your site meets Google's standards.

The good news is that Google tells you what is wrong. When your site is rejected, you receive an email and a notification in your AdSense account explaining the reason. By reading and understanding this feedback carefully, you can make the necessary improvements and reapply with confidence.

The Most Common Reasons for AdSense Rejection

1. Low Value Content

This is the single most common rejection reason. It means Google believes your site does not have enough high-quality, original, and useful content. Sites with very few pages, short articles, copied content, or content that does not genuinely help readers will receive this rejection. The fix is to add more detailed, original content across multiple pages of your site.

2. Insufficient Content

Similar to low value content but more specifically about quantity. Your site simply does not have enough pages or articles for Google to evaluate properly. As a general rule, aim for at least 15 to 20 substantial pages before reapplying.

3. Site Under Construction

If your site looks unfinished — missing pages, placeholder text, broken layouts — Google will reject it. Your site must be fully functional and complete before you apply. Every link should work, every page should have real content, and the design should be clean and professional.

4. No Privacy Policy

Google requires all AdSense publishers to have a Privacy Policy page. This page must explain what data your site collects and how it is used. Without it, your application will be rejected regardless of how good your content is.

5. Policy Violations

If your site contains prohibited content — adult material, violent content, content promoting illegal activities, or misleading content — it will be rejected and may be permanently barred from AdSense. Always review Google's Program Policies before applying.

6. Domain Ownership Issues

If Google cannot verify that you own the domain, your application cannot proceed. Make sure the AdSense verification code or ads.txt file is properly added to your site.

⚠️ Important: Do not click "Request Review" until you have genuinely fixed all the issues. Submitting too many reviews without making real improvements can slow down future reviews and signal to Google that you are not taking the policies seriously.

How to Read Google's Rejection Feedback

When your site is rejected, log into your AdSense account and go to the Sites section. Click on your site to see the specific policy violations listed. Each violation will have a brief description and usually links to more detailed guidance from Google.

Read each rejection reason carefully and follow the linked resources. Google's help pages explain exactly what they are looking for and what changes will satisfy their requirements. Do not guess — read the guidelines and implement the specific changes they describe.

The Step-by-Step Fix and Resubmit Process

Step 1 — Read the rejection reason carefully. Understand exactly what Google is flagging. Is it content quality? Missing pages? Policy violations? Each problem has a different solution.

Step 2 — Make all necessary improvements. Do not rush this step. Take time to genuinely improve your site. Add more detailed content, create missing pages, fix navigation issues, or remove policy-violating content — whatever the rejection reason requires.

Step 3 — Review your entire site before resubmitting. Go through every page of your site as if you are a Google reviewer seeing it for the first time. Is every page helpful and complete? Is navigation clear? Are all essential pages present and linked?

Step 4 — Confirm and request a new review. Once you are confident all issues are fixed, go to the Sites page in AdSense, tick the checkbox that says "I confirm I have fixed the issues," and click "Request Review." Only do this when you are truly ready.

Step 5 — Wait patiently. Reviews typically take one to two weeks. Avoid submitting multiple review requests during this waiting period. Use the time to continue improving your site and adding more content.

✅ Final Pre-Submission Checklist:

✔ At least 15 pages of original, detailed content
✔ Each page has 600+ words of quality content
✔ Privacy Policy page exists and is linked from every page
✔ About page exists
✔ Contact page exists
✔ Clear navigation menu on every page
✔ No broken links or missing pages
✔ Site is mobile-friendly
✔ No copied or plagiarized content
✔ No prohibited content
✔ AdSense code or ads.txt file properly added
✔ Site loads quickly and without errors

How Long Before You Can Reapply?

There is no mandatory waiting period before resubmitting your site for review after rejection. However, it is strongly recommended to wait until you have made substantial, genuine improvements. Submitting too quickly after making only minor changes is likely to result in another rejection, which wastes time and slows down the overall process.

Conclusion

AdSense rejection is a temporary setback, not a permanent barrier. By understanding exactly why your site was rejected, making genuine and thorough improvements, and resubmitting only when your site is truly ready, you give yourself the best possible chance of approval. Remember that Google wants to approve good websites — their business depends on having quality publishers. Build a quality site and approval will follow.
