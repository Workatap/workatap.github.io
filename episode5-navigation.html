Navigation: How Your Site Structure Affects AdSense Approval and User Experience

Published on WorkAtAp | Building a well-structured website that Google and visitors love

When Google reviews your website for AdSense approval, it does not only look at your content. It also evaluates how easy your site is to navigate and use. Poor navigation is one of the overlooked reasons why many sites fail their AdSense review. In this episode, we explain what good website navigation looks like, why it matters, and how to improve your site's structure to satisfy both Google and your visitors.

What Is Website Navigation?

Website navigation refers to the system of menus, links, and structure that allows visitors to move around your website easily. Think of it like the layout of a building — good navigation means visitors can find what they are looking for quickly and without confusion. Bad navigation means visitors get lost, cannot find important pages, and leave your site frustrated.

For AdSense purposes, navigation matters because Google wants ads to appear on sites that provide a good user experience. If a site is confusing or difficult to use, it reflects poorly on the advertisers whose ads appear on it. Google therefore checks that your site is well-organized and user-friendly as part of the approval process.

The Main Navigation Menu

Every website should have a clear main navigation menu — usually a row of links at the top of every page. This menu should include links to the most important sections of your site. For a blog or content site like an AdSense episode guide, a good main menu typically includes links to the Home page, an About page, a Contact page, and major content categories.

Your navigation menu should appear consistently on every page of your site. Visitors should never be on a page where they cannot find a way back to the homepage or to other sections of your site. Consistency in navigation builds trust and makes your site feel professional.

Essential Pages Every Site Must Have

Beyond the main content pages, there are several essential pages that every AdSense-approved site should have. These pages are not just good practice — Google specifically looks for them during the review process.

Home page — Your home page should clearly explain what your site is about and give visitors easy access to your best or most recent content. A confusing or empty home page will cause visitors to leave immediately.

About page — The About page tells visitors who you are and why you created this site. It adds a human element to your site and builds trust. Google's reviewers also use this page to understand the purpose of your site.

Contact page — A contact page provides visitors and Google with a way to reach you. It can be as simple as a contact form or an email address. Having a contact page shows that your site is transparent and accountable.

Privacy Policy page — This is arguably the most important page for AdSense approval. Google requires that all AdSense publishers have a Privacy Policy that explains how visitor data is collected and used. Without this page, your site will almost certainly be rejected.

💡 Important: Your Privacy Policy, About, and Contact pages must be easily reachable from any page on your site — ideally linked in both the main navigation menu and the footer.

Internal Linking

Internal linking refers to links within your content that point to other pages on your own site. For example, at the end of Episode 1, there is a link to Episode 2. This is internal linking. Good internal linking helps visitors discover more of your content and keeps them on your site longer.

From a technical standpoint, internal links also help Google understand the structure of your site and which pages are most important. A well-linked site is easier for Google to crawl and index, which can improve your site's visibility in search results.

Mobile-Friendly Navigation

More than half of all web traffic now comes from mobile devices — smartphones and tablets. If your site's navigation does not work well on small screens, you are providing a poor experience for a large portion of your visitors. Google takes mobile friendliness seriously and uses it as a factor in both search rankings and AdSense reviews.

For a GitHub Pages site, make sure your navigation menu is readable and usable on a small screen. Avoid menus that are too wide to fit on a phone screen. Ideally, your menu should stack vertically on mobile devices or use a simple hamburger menu icon.

Page Loading Speed

Navigation is not only about menus and links — it is also about how quickly your pages load. If a visitor clicks a link and the next page takes many seconds to load, they will likely give up and leave. Fast page loading is part of good user experience and is something Google evaluates.

For GitHub Pages sites, speed is generally good because GitHub's servers are fast. However, avoid adding very large images or heavy scripts that slow down your pages unnecessarily.

✅ Navigation Checklist:

✔ Main navigation menu on every page
✔ Home page clearly explains the site's purpose
✔ About page exists and is linked in navigation
✔ Contact page exists and is linked in navigation
✔ Privacy Policy page exists and is linked in footer
✔ Internal links connect related pages
✔ Navigation works on mobile devices
✔ All links work correctly (no broken links)
✔ Pages load quickly

Conclusion

Good website navigation is essential for both your visitors and Google's AdSense reviewers. A well-structured site with a clear menu, essential pages, good internal linking, and mobile-friendly design creates a positive user experience that Google rewards with ad approval. Take time to review your site's navigation before applying or reapplying for AdSense — small improvements in structure can make a significant difference in your approval chances.
