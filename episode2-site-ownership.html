Site Ownership and Basic Checks: What Google Verifies Before Approving Your Site

Published on WorkAtAp | Understanding the AdSense verification process

Before Google AdSense approves your website to show ads, it goes through a careful review process. One of the first and most important steps is verifying that you actually own the site you are applying with. This process is called site ownership verification. In this episode, we explain what site ownership verification means, how it works, and what other basic checks Google performs on your site during the AdSense review.

What Is Site Ownership Verification?

Site ownership verification is the process by which Google confirms that you are the legitimate owner or administrator of the website you are trying to connect to AdSense. This step exists to prevent people from claiming someone else's website and earning money from ads placed on it without the real owner's knowledge or permission.

Google takes this step very seriously. Without proper verification, anyone could enter any website address into AdSense and try to profit from traffic they do not own. Verification protects website owners and ensures that AdSense earnings go to the right person.

How Does Google Verify Site Ownership?

Google offers several methods to verify that you own your site. The most common method for websites and blogs is adding a small piece of code to your website. Here is how it works:

Method 1 — AdSense Code Snippet: When you add your site to AdSense, Google gives you a unique code snippet. You paste this code into the HTML of your website, typically in the section between the opening and closing head tags. Once Google detects this code on your site, ownership is confirmed.

Method 2 — ads.txt File: Google may also ask you to add an ads.txt file to your website's root directory. This is a simple text file that declares which advertising networks are authorized to sell ads on your site. Adding this file also serves as proof of ownership because only the site owner can add files to the root directory.

Method 3 — Google Search Console: If your site is already verified in Google Search Console, AdSense can use that existing verification. This is another reason why connecting your site to Google Search Console early is a good practice for any website owner.

💡 Tip for GitHub Pages users: To add the AdSense code to a GitHub Pages site, open your repository, find your main HTML file or layout template, and paste the AdSense code just before the closing </head> tag. Then commit and push the changes. Google will detect the code within a few days.

What Basic Checks Does Google Perform?

Beyond ownership verification, Google's review team checks several other aspects of your site before granting AdSense approval. Understanding these checks helps you prepare your site properly and avoid rejection.

Check 1 — Is the site accessible? Google checks whether your website is publicly accessible to anyone on the internet. If your site is password-protected, set to private, or returns error pages, Google cannot review it and will not approve it. Make sure your site loads correctly before applying.

Check 2 — Does the site have enough content? Google reviewers look at how much content your site has. A site with only one or two pages, or pages with very little text, will likely be flagged as low value content. You need multiple pages with detailed, helpful written content.

Check 3 — Is the content original? Google checks whether your content is copied from other websites. Duplicate content — content that appears word-for-word on other sites — will cause your application to be rejected. All your content must be original and written by you.

Check 4 — Does the site follow AdSense policies? Google checks whether your site contains any prohibited content such as adult material, violent content, content promoting illegal activities, or content that encourages people to click ads artificially. Any such content will result in rejection.

Check 5 — Does the site have a clear purpose? Google wants to see that your site serves a clear purpose and provides value to visitors. Sites that appear to exist only to display ads, with no real purpose or audience, will not be approved.

How Long Does the Verification and Review Take?

After you submit your site and the ownership code is detected, Google's review team will evaluate your site. This process typically takes between one day and two weeks, though in some cases it can take longer. You will receive an email from Google once the review is complete, telling you whether your site has been approved or whether there are issues to fix.

✅ Site Readiness Checklist Before Applying:

✔ AdSense code added to all pages
✔ Site is publicly accessible (not private or password-protected)
✔ At least 10 pages of original, detailed content
✔ Privacy Policy page exists
✔ About page exists
✔ Contact page exists
✔ No prohibited or copied content
✔ Site loads without errors

What Happens After Verification?

Once ownership is verified and your site passes the basic checks, your site moves into the full content and policy review. This is where Google evaluates the quality and depth of your content, your site's navigation and user experience, and whether your site meets all program policies.

If your site passes everything, you will be approved and can start showing ads immediately. If there are issues, Google will tell you what needs to be fixed and you can request a new review after making improvements.

Conclusion

Site ownership verification is a simple but essential step in the AdSense application process. By adding the AdSense code to your site and ensuring your website is publicly accessible with quality original content, you will pass this stage with no problems. The basic checks Google performs are all things within your control — as long as your site is genuine, well-structured, and filled with helpful content, you are on the right path to approval.
